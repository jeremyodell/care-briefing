# Rheumatoid Arthritis

## Standard of Care

Care follows a treat-to-target strategy: set a goal of remission (or low disease activity if remission isn't achievable) and adjust therapy on a regular schedule until that target is met, per the [2021 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) Guideline for the Treatment of Rheumatoid Arthritis](https://acrjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/art.41752). Methotrexate remains the "anchor drug": for DMARD-naive patients with moderate-to-high disease activity, ACR strongly recommends methotrexate monotherapy over starting a biologic or targeted synthetic DMARD, and over hydroxychloroquine or sulfasalazine ([ACR 2021 guideline](https://acrjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/art.41752); [Healio summary](https://www.healio.com/news/rheumatology/20210811/methotrexate-mainstay-acr-guidelines-reaffirm-old-dmard-as-anchor-drug-in-ra-therapy)). The guideline also carries a strong recommendation to minimize glucocorticoid use ([AAFP summary of ACR update](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2022/0900/practice-guidelines-rheumatoid-arthritis)).

If disease activity remains moderate-to-high despite methotrexate, ACR recommends adding a biologic DMARD or a targeted synthetic DMARD (tsDMARD) rather than switching to triple conventional-DMARD therapy ([ACR 2021 guideline](https://acrjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/art.41752)). Biologic options include TNF inhibitors (e.g., adalimumab, etanercept), IL-6 inhibitors (e.g., tocilizumab), abatacept, and rituximab; tsDMARDs are the oral JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib/Xeljanz, baricitinib/Olumiant, upadacitinib/Rinvoq) ([drugs.com overview of current RA drug classes](https://www.drugs.com/medical-answers/new-drugs-treatment-rheumatoid-arthritis-3512243/)).

A major guideline-shaping safety signal: in September 2021 the FDA required boxed-warning revisions across all JAK inhibitors approved for RA, based on the ORAL Surveillance postmarketing trial, which found higher rates of major cardiovascular events, malignancy, venous thromboembolism, and death with tofacitinib versus TNF inhibitors in patients 50+ with a cardiovascular risk factor ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/rheumatology/20210901/fda-adds-black-box-warning-to-jak-inhibitors-cites-heartrelated-issues-cancer-death); [ACR boxed-warning statement](https://rheumatology.org/api/asset/bltd58974433694d161)). As a result, FDA restricted JAK-inhibitor use (across tofacitinib, baricitinib, and upadacitinib) to patients who have had an inadequate response to one or more TNF inhibitors, pushing JAK inhibitors later in the treatment sequence ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/rheumatology/20210901/fda-adds-black-box-warning-to-jak-inhibitors-cites-heartrelated-issues-cancer-death)).

For patients who reach sustained low disease activity or remission (6+ months), ACR conditionally recommends tapering rather than abruptly stopping therapy, in this order: continue all DMARDs at current dose, then reduce DMARD dose, then reduce-and-gradually-discontinue, with abrupt discontinuation last ([ACR 2021 guideline](https://acrjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/art.41752)).

## Emerging Treatments in Development

**TLL-018 (JAK1/TYK2 inhibitor, Hangzhou Highlightll Pharmaceutical)** — an oral dual JAK1/TYK2 inhibitor designed to spare JAK2/JAK3, aiming to preserve efficacy while reducing the safety issues tied to less-selective JAK inhibitors. A head-to-head Phase 2a study showed higher ACR50 response rates for TLL-018 (20mg/30mg doses) than tofacitinib at week 12 ([ACR Meeting Abstracts](https://acrabstracts.org/abstract/head-to-head-comparison-of-tll-018-and-tofacitinib-in-patients-with-active-rheumatoid-arthritis-final-results-from-a-phase-iia-study/)). It has advanced to a Phase 3 trial comparing TLL-018 directly against tofacitinib in patients with inadequate response/intolerance to biologic DMARDs ([NCT06020144](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06020144)), with a Phase 3 long-term extension study also recruiting ([NCT06887127](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06887127)).

**Tulisokibart / MK-7240 (anti-TL1A monoclonal antibody, Merck/MSD)** — originally developed for inflammatory bowel disease, tulisokibart binds and neutralizes TNF-like cytokine 1A (TL1A), a cytokine implicated in multiple immune-mediated inflammatory diseases. Merck began a global Phase 2b RA trial (targeting 640+ patients) in October 2025, with primary completion estimated September 2026 ([European Pharmaceutical Review](https://www.europeanpharmaceuticalreview.com/news/266817/merck-co-phase-iib-clinical-trial-tulisokibart-rheumatoid-arthritis/); [NCT07176390](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07176390)).

**Early-stage pipeline (Phase 1/2–2, recruiting):** Novartis's rapcabtagene autoleucel, a cellular therapy, in RA and Sjögren's ([NCT07048197](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07048197)); Bristol Myers Squibb's BMS-986528 in refractory RA ([NCT07412704](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07412704)); and AstraZeneca's AZD1163 ([NCT07276581](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07276581)). Celltrion's CT-P13 SC (subcutaneous infliximab biosimilar) is in Phase 3 for moderate-to-severe RA ([NCT06738719](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06738719)).

**Newly FDA-approved, non-drug modality:** In July 2025 the FDA approved the SetPoint System, the first neuroimmune-modulation (vagus nerve stimulation) device for RA, for adults with moderate-to-severe RA who have had an inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to biologic/tsDMARDs. The implanted device stimulates the vagus nerve daily to reduce TNF and other inflammatory proteins via the spleen; approval was supported by the 242-patient RESET-RA sham-controlled trial showing significant ACR20 improvement at week 12 ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/rheumatology/20250731/fda-approves-vagus-nerve-stimulator-for-rheumatoid-arthritis); [SetPoint Medical press release](https://setpointmedical.com/setpoint-medical-receives-fda-approval-for-novel-neuroimmune-modulation-therapy-for-rheumatoid-arthritis/)).

**Biosimilar expansion:** On March 5, 2024, FDA approved Tyenne (tocilizumab-aazg, Fresenius Kabi), the first tocilizumab biosimilar available in both IV and subcutaneous formulations, expanding lower-cost access to IL-6 inhibition for RA ([Fresenius Kabi](https://www.fresenius-kabi.com/us/news-and-events/fresenius-kabi-s-biosimilar-tyenne---tocilizumab-aazg--becomes-t); [PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-approves-fresenius-kabi-s-tyenne-a-biosimilar-of-actemra-for-the-treatment-of-autoimmune-diseases)).

## Key Companies & Institutions

**AbbVie, Pfizer, and Eli Lilly** market the three FDA-approved JAK inhibitors for RA — Rinvoq (upadacitinib), Xeljanz (tofacitinib), and Olumiant (baricitinib), respectively — all now carrying the 2021 boxed warning ([drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/medical-answers/new-drugs-treatment-rheumatoid-arthritis-3512243/); [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/rheumatology/20210901/fda-adds-black-box-warning-to-jak-inhibitors-cites-heartrelated-issues-cancer-death)).

**Merck/MSD** sponsors the Phase 2b tulisokibart (anti-TL1A) RA program ([NCT07176390](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07176390)). **Hangzhou Highlightll Pharmaceutical** (China) is advancing the JAK1/TYK2 inhibitor TLL-018 through Phase 3 ([NCT06020144](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06020144)). **Novartis**, **Bristol Myers Squibb**, and **AstraZeneca** each sponsor early-phase RA pipeline candidates — rapcabtagene autoleucel, BMS-986528, and AZD1163 respectively ([NCT07048197](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07048197); [NCT07412704](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07412704); [NCT07276581](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07276581)). **Celltrion** sponsors the Phase 3 CT-P13 SC biosimilar trial ([NCT06738719](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06738719)), and **Fresenius Kabi** brought the first dual IV/SC tocilizumab biosimilar (Tyenne) to market in 2024 ([Fresenius Kabi](https://www.fresenius-kabi.com/us/news-and-events/fresenius-kabi-s-biosimilar-tyenne---tocilizumab-aazg--becomes-t)). **SetPoint Medical** developed the first FDA-approved neuroimmune-modulation device for RA ([SetPoint Medical](https://setpointmedical.com/setpoint-medical-receives-fda-approval-for-novel-neuroimmune-modulation-therapy-for-rheumatoid-arthritis/)).

**NIH/NIAMS** funded the Accelerating Medicines Partnership RA/SLE network (2014–2022) — 11 academic groups profiling immune and tissue cells in RA and lupus to identify drug targets — now continued as the broader AMP Autoimmune and Immune-Mediated Diseases (AMP AIM) program ([NIAMS: AMP RA/SLE](https://www.niams.nih.gov/grants-funding/funded-research/accelerating-medicines/overview); [NIAMS: AMP AIM](https://www.niams.nih.gov/grants-funding/niams-supported-research-programs/accelerating-medicines-partnership-amp)).
