# Multiple Myeloma

## Standard of Care

For transplant-eligible, newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM), induction therapy has shifted from three-drug to four-drug (quadruplet) regimens: a CD38-targeted monoclonal antibody (daratumumab or isatuximab) added to bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (Dara-VRd or Isa-VRd), with VRd triplet remaining an acceptable option for patients who cannot tolerate a quadruplet ([Dana-Farber Cancer Institute](https://www.dana-farber.org/cancer-care/types/multiple-myeloma/treatment); [PMC review, induction pre-ASCT](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8960754/); [IMF 2026 FAQ with Dr. S. Vincent Rajkumar](https://www.myeloma.org/blog/updated-faq-newly-diagnosed-myeloma-2026)). Following induction, autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) after high-dose melphalan remains standard for eligible patients, with eligibility weighed against age, frailty, comorbidities, response to induction, and patient goals of care ([Dana-Farber](https://www.dana-farber.org/cancer-care/types/multiple-myeloma/treatment); [PMC, role of ASCT in the immunotherapy era](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11120591/)). Post-transplant (or post-induction for non-transplant patients), lenalidomide is the most common maintenance therapy, continued until progression or intolerance; a proteasome inhibitor such as bortezomib or carfilzomib is an alternative ([PMC, maintenance strategies post-ASCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8432350/); [Dana-Farber](https://www.dana-farber.org/cancer-care/types/multiple-myeloma/treatment)).

For relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM), two classes of immunotherapy have become central: BCMA-directed CAR T-cell therapies (idecabtagene vicleucel/Abecma and ciltacabtagene autoleucel/Carvykti), and T-cell-engaging bispecific antibodies targeting BCMA, GPRC5D, or other tumor antigens (e.g., teclistamab, talquetamab, elranatamab, linvoseltamab) ([PMC, CAR-T in RRMM](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11340289/); [PMC, systematic review of bispecifics in RRMM](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10294881/)). Meta-analysis data suggest CAR-T achieves higher complete-response rates than bispecifics (pooled CR ~54% vs. ~35%) as later-line therapy, while bispecifics carry a higher relative rate of cytopenias ([PubMed, CAR-T vs. bispecific meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39551604/)).

## Emerging Treatments in Development

**MonumenTAL-3** (NCT05455320, Phase 3, sponsored by Johnson & Johnson) is testing talquetamab (a GPRC5D-targeted bispecific) combined with daratumumab, with or without pomalidomide, versus daratumumab-pomalidomide-dexamethasone in RRMM patients with ≥1 prior line of therapy; results were reported in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in 2026 ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2604657); [Health Research Authority summary](https://www.hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/application-summaries/research-summaries/monumental-3/)).

Bristol Myers Squibb is advancing two oral CELMoD agents in Phase 3 RRMM trials: **mezigdomide** (SUCCESSOR-2, NCT05552976), combined with carfilzomib and dexamethasone, which showed a 52% reduction in risk of progression or death versus standard therapy in interim results ([BMS press release](https://news.bms.com/news/corporate-financial/2026/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-Announces-CELMoD-Mezigdomide-Reduces-Risk-of-Disease-Progression-or-Death-by-More-than-50-vs--Standard-of-Care-in-Relapsed-or-Refractory-Multiple-Myeloma/default.aspx)); and **iberdomide** (EXCALIBER-RRMM), combined with daratumumab and dexamethasone, which met its interim endpoint for improved minimal residual disease negativity ([BMS press release](https://news.bms.com/news/corporate-financial/2025/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-Announces-Phase-3-EXCALIBER-RRMM-Study-Evaluating-Iberdomide-in-Combination-with-Standard-Therapies-Demonstrated-a-Significant-Improvement-in-Minimal-Residual-Disease-Negativity-Rates-in-Relapsed-or-Refractory-Multiple-Myeloma/default.aspx)).

Recent FDA approvals have expanded the RRMM armamentarium: **linvoseltamab-gcpt (Lynozyfic)**, a BCMA-CD3 bispecific from Regeneron, received accelerated approval July 2, 2025 for patients with ≥4 prior lines of therapy ([FDA approval notice](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-linvoseltamab-gcpt-relapsed-or-refractory-multiple-myeloma); [PMC review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12631538/)); and **belantamab mafodotin (Blenrep)**, a BCMA-targeted antibody-drug conjugate from GSK, was approved in combination with bortezomib/dexamethasone in October 2025 based on the DREAMM-7 trial ([Blood Cancer United](https://bloodcancerunited.org/resources/newsroom/fda-approves-drug-people-advanced-disease-multiple-myeloma)).

## Key Companies & Institutions

**Johnson & Johnson/Janssen** develops daratumumab (Darzalex), teclistamab (Tecvayli), and talquetamab (Talvey), and co-developed ciltacabtagene autoleucel (Carvykti) with **Legend Biotech**; it sponsors MonumenTAL-3 ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2604657)). **Bristol Myers Squibb** markets idecabtagene vicleucel (Abecma) and sponsors the mezigdomide and iberdomide Phase 3 programs ([BMS newsroom](https://news.bms.com/news/corporate-financial/2026/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-Announces-Positive-Phase-3-Results-from-the-SUCCESSOR-2-Study-of-Oral-Mezigdomide-in-Relapsed-or-Refractory-Multiple-Myeloma/default.aspx)). **Regeneron** developed linvoseltamab (Lynozyfic); **GSK** developed belantamab mafodotin (Blenrep) and ran the DREAMM-7 trial ([Blood Cancer United](https://bloodcancerunited.org/resources/newsroom/fda-approves-drug-people-advanced-disease-multiple-myeloma)); **Pfizer** developed elranatamab (Elrexfio), studied in the MagnetisMM-3 trial (compound PF-06863135) ([ClinicalTrials.gov protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/59/NCT04649359/Prot_000.pdf)); **Sanofi** markets isatuximab (Sarclisa), used in quadruplet induction. Academic centers **Dana-Farber Cancer Institute**, **Memorial Sloan Kettering**, and **Mayo Clinic** are prominent trial sites and sources of NCCN-aligned patient-education content on current standard of care ([Dana-Farber](https://www.dana-farber.org/cancer-care/types/multiple-myeloma/treatment)).
